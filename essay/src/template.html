01 / FOLLOW THE ADDITION

The powers kept growing.
Their digits overlapped.

Because 997 = 1000 − 3, its reciprocal is a geometric series:

1997= 11000+ 31000²+ 91000³+…

Each numerator triples while its place moves three decimal digits to the right. The amounts get smaller, and we add them. Once a power needs more room, its digits reach back into earlier groups. Check the identity.

The progression continues forever. To find the repeating part, we can keep much less information: just the leftover from each division.

02 / FOLLOW THE LEFTOVER

The leftover remembers
what comes next.

At every position, dividing the growing power by 997 leaves the same remainder that enters the long-division step. Multiplying by 1000 or by 3 leaves the same remainder: the difference is exactly 997 times the starting number.

To print three digits, multiply the remainder by 1000, divide by 997, and keep the new leftover. The powers keep growing; the remainders stay below 997. Here they follow a loop of 166 group steps. Once a remainder returns, every later group repeats too.

A second discovery: the same loop writes in base 3

Look at the small counts from splitting three times a remainder: each is 0, 1, or 2. Those are also the digits of 1/997 in base 3. The registered digit-periodicity result supplies the division rule; the two-readout identity below is a specialized algebraic derivation.

Beyond geometric series: the general carry identity

These are algebraic derivations for the broader recurrence engine. Separate formalization of the full recurrence framework remains open; see the proof-status atlas for registered claims.

Let B ≥ 2 and D ≥ 2 be integers, and choose coherent integer states x and x′ such that Bx − x′ is divisible by D. Define the raw emission a = (Bx − x′)/D. Write the Euclidean divisions x = Dq + r and x′ = Dq′ + r′, with 0 ≤ r, r′ < D.

w = a + q′ − Bq
Br = Dw + r′

The second line is ordinary long division. The first describes exactly how any coherent integer lift yields its printed group. Taking x = r and x′ = r′ gives the canonical lift with both quotient coordinates zero. A recurrence-adapted lift instead exposes simple growing terms.

For a monic polynomial P(X) = Xd − c1Xd−1 − … − cd with integer coefficients, d ≥ 1, and D = P(B) > 1, reduce UXm modulo P and evaluate at B. These lifted states produce the seeded recurrence, while Euclidean normalization produces the same groups as division of U/D. Here U is an integer with 0 < U < D, and the initial state is x0 = U.

Starting from x0 = U in [0, D), the first changed group occurs at the first step whose next lifted state leaves [0, D), if such a step occurs. Until that exit, both quotient coordinates are zero; at the exit, q′ becomes nonzero while q is still zero. This need not be exactly one group before a raw term overflows. Signed states require floor division and may produce borrows.

The general finite-prefix certificate

After M terms, let A = a0BM−1 + ⋯ + aM−1 be the raw prefix read as one weighted integer, and xM its remaining lifted state. With initial state x0 = U:

D × A + xM = U × BM

The fraction U/D is exactly A/BM + xM/(D × BM). Writing xM = DqM + rM, with 0 ≤ rM < D, settles the prefix to (A + qM)/BM and leaves rM/(D × BM).

The display can therefore stop without pretending the unwritten balance is zero. This finite identity needs no infinite-series convergence assumption. Interpreting the raw terms as an infinite sum does require convergence; the positive examples on this page satisfy it.

OPTIONAL CONTINUATION / MULTIPLICATION

What if multiplication
were something you could see?

Multiplying two or three geometric series gives another view of the same principle: collect the contributions at each place, then carry.

Open the square and cube geometry＋

Start with another simple list: 1/9999 = 0.0001 0001 0001 …. We use four-digit groups because 9999 is one less than 10000.

Squaring pairs every term in one copy with every term in another. A pair from positions 2 and 3 lands in position 5. The pairs form a grid; pairs with the same sum lie along a diagonal.

Counting numbers count how many products arrive at each place. Cubing adds a third choice. The resulting slices contain triangular numbers: 1, 3, 6, 10, …

For the square, the raw four-digit groups begin 0000, 0001, 0002, 0003, …. For the cube, they begin 0000, 0000, 0001, 0003, 0006, 0010, ….

The initial zeros have a reason: two positive positions cannot sum to 1, and three cannot sum to 1 or 2. These early coefficients fit unchanged; later contributions require carrying too.

From a grid to a coefficient

Each factor is the convergent geometric series Σᵢ≥₁ B⁻ⁱ, with B = 10000. Multiplication collects ordered pairs i + j = s, or ordered triples i + j + k = s. There are s − 1 pairs and (s − 1)(s − 2)/2 triples at place s, once their respective starting positions are reached.

This is the Cauchy product of absolutely convergent series. Collecting contributions produces raw coefficients; carrying normalizes those coefficients into ordinary base-B groups. They are separate operations.
