The original source SHA256 is `ff097c71984069ab4fcae033aa86240ee3d572d14431e7c47d76d3ef322039a9`. Its eleven theorem statements and proof bodies are unchanged. The published module SHA256 is `9981530ff4218b2cee8c8877ce1651095a177408c7495ac70f6e499186022cb4`.

The original phase9 modules remain unchanged except that the `RadixRuns.lean` import barrel now includes `CarryBridge` and `HandoffSearch`. `RadixResearch-source-hashes.json` records the changed barrel and added modules; all other existing hash entries are preserved.

Run `python3 check_radix_proofs.py` from `lean/` to build and verify the 24 recorded source hashes and 199 theorem axiom checks. Only the standard logical dependencies `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` are permitted. These proofs establish the mathematical identities independently; they are not formal verification of the browser's JavaScript implementation.

This addition proves the adaptive positive-digit schedule, local radix minimality with a positive residual, prefix preservation, and convergence of the infinite continuation. It includes explicit steps for 1/997 and 1/998001. The examples' starting prefixes are independently checked by the browser model's tests; the general Lean theorem `adaptive_after_prefix_hasSum` accepts their exact prefix identity as its hypothesis. It does not claim a uniquely preferred progression, a universal construction for zero target digits, or a globally shortest sequence of handoffs.

The bracket and bar typography is explanatory notation. Run records, generated digits, value preservation, first-return certificates and convergence are formalized. No notation parser or rule selecting a unique preferred progression is formalized.
